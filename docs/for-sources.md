# If you are thinking about sending something

This page is for you. It is not technical. Please read all of it — it takes
about five minutes, and the mistakes it describes are the ones that actually get
people caught.

---

## The most important thing

**Cryptography is not your main risk.** In nearly every case where a source has
been identified, the failure was ordinary: they used a work computer, they were
one of six people with access to a document, they searched for the journalist's
name beforehand, or the document itself carried an invisible watermark.

Aletheia protects the *file*. You have to protect *you*.

## Before you do anything

1. **Not on a work device. Not on a work network.** Employers log far more than
   people expect, and the log is the first thing examined. Use a personal
   device, on a network that is not yours and not your employer's — a library,
   a café, somewhere you do not normally go.
2. **Do not research this first from your own account.** Looking up the
   newsroom, this site, or "how to leak safely" from a traceable account
   establishes intent and timing before you have even started.
3. **Consider Tor.** The onion address is on the front page. If the fact that
   you visited this site at all is dangerous, use it.
4. **Think about who else could have this.** If only three people can access
   what you are sending, no technology makes you anonymous. In that situation,
   talk to a journalist about it before sending anything — there are editorial
   techniques for handling small-population material, and they need to be
   planned in advance.

## About the file itself

- **Photographs and videos from a modern camera or phone may carry a signature**
  that proves they are real. That is good — it is what lets a newsroom trust
  what you send. It also contains where and when and on what device. Aletheia
  lets you keep the proof and drop the rest.
- **Documents from an organisation may be watermarked** in ways you cannot see:
  invisible spacing patterns, per-recipient wording changes, a unique printer
  pattern of yellow dots on a scan. Aletheia cannot detect or remove these.
  Retyping a document removes them, at the cost of the proof of authenticity.
  This is a real trade-off and only you can weigh it.
- **Screenshots carry your screen, your theme, your open tabs, your username in
  the corner.** Crop before you send, and check the crop.

## What we ask you to choose

You will be shown each thing that could be published about your file, and you
choose how precise it is. For example:

| You can say | Or narrow it to | Or say nothing |
|---|---|---|
| Somewhere in Europe | Poland | (region hidden) |
| In 2026 | March 2026 | (date hidden) |
| Taken on a real camera | Taken on a Sony camera | (device hidden) |

The interface shows a number beside each choice: **how many people your choice
is consistent with.** If a combination narrows to fewer than a thousand, the
screen turns amber and tells you plainly. If it narrows to fewer than a hundred,
it asks you to reconsider before continuing.

More precision helps the journalist. Less precision protects you. Nobody but you
can make that trade, so we give you the number rather than a recommendation.

## What happens after you send

- The file goes to the newsroom encrypted. Reading it needs several editors to
  agree, not one.
- The proof is published so anyone can check the file is genuine.
- You get a claim code. Keep it somewhere safe and offline. It lets you send a
  follow-up message that the newsroom knows came from the same source, without
  either of you ever knowing who the other is.
- **Do not tell anyone you did this.** Not a partner, not a friend, not a
  colleague you trust. This is the most commonly ignored piece of advice and the
  most reliable way people are caught.

## If something goes wrong

There is no undo. Once a proof is published it is public and permanent. Before
you press the last button, the interface shows you exactly what will be
published, in full, in plain words, and asks you to confirm. Read that screen
slowly. It is the last moment at which any of this is reversible.

## Getting help first

If you are unsure, contact a journalist before you send anything. Many
newsrooms will talk to a potential source about *whether* and *how* to send
before any material changes hands. That conversation is often the single most
protective step available to you.
